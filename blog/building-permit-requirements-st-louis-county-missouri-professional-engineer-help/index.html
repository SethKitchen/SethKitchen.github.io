Building Permit Requirements in St. Louis County, Missouri and How a Professional Engineer Can Help

August 6, 2026

If you are planning construction, renovation, or system upgrades in St. Louis County, Missouri, confirming the correct permitting authority is an important first step. St. Louis County administers building permits for unincorporated areas and for many municipalities that contract with the county for code enforcement — but that is not the same as the City of St. Louis, and not every local city uses the county for every review.

For some projects, involvement from a licensed Professional Engineer (PE) can help clarify technical requirements, prepare sealed drawings when required, and support plan review.

This article provides general information about building permit requirements in St. Louis County, Missouri. It does not constitute legal or engineering advice or establish a professional engagement. Always confirm current requirements with the applicable building department before starting work.

County vs. City: Confirming Jurisdiction First

The St. Louis metro area has several overlapping review paths. Confirm which applies to your address before preparing a full submittal package:

  • Unincorporated St. Louis County — Building permits are typically handled through St. Louis County Department of Transportation and Public Works (code enforcement / permit processing)
  • Municipalities that contract with the county — Many St. Louis County cities use county building permits, but still require local zoning, occupancy, or other municipal approvals before or alongside the county filing
  • City of St. Louis — A separate city permitting system; do not assume county Accela covers city addresses
  • Cities with their own building departments — Some municipalities handle building permits independently; verify before submitting to the county portal

Parcel maps, annexation history, and municipal contracting arrangements matter. If you are unsure whether the county reviews your address, contact the local city hall and St. Louis County permit processing before ordering drawings or equipment.

Why Building Permits Are Required

Permits are intended to verify that proposed work meets adopted codes and local ordinances. The process helps protect:

  • Structural safety and fire resistance
  • Proper installation of electrical, mechanical, and plumbing systems
  • Occupant health and public welfare
  • Clear records for future buyers, lenders, and insurers

Working without a required permit can lead to stop-work orders, corrective construction, difficulty closing permits later, and complications at sale or during insurance claims.

Accela Citizen Access and the Online Permit Process

St. Louis County uses Accela Citizen Access as its online permitting and contractor licensing portal (commonly reached through the county's permitting portal). Through Accela, applicants can often:

  • Apply for construction permits
  • Upload plans and supporting documents
  • Track applications, review comments, and issued permits
  • Pay fees and schedule inspections
  • Apply for related items such as occupancy inspections, contractor licensing, or transportation permits when applicable

First-time users generally need a portal account. County training materials emphasize choosing the correct construction record type at the start — staff typically cannot change an incorrect type, and applicants may need to reapply. Common record-type categories include existing building work, non-habitable accessory work, and new building construction.

For questions about submissions, account setup, or project-specific requirements, contact St. Louis County permit processing through the Department of Transportation and Public Works channels published on the county website (commonly (314) 615-5184 for permit and inspection inquiries). Confirm the current phone and office hours before relying on them.

Common Types of Permits

St. Louis County typically issues several categories of permits depending on the project:

Building Permits

Required for work such as:

  • New residential or commercial construction
  • Additions, remodels, and structural modifications
  • Decks, garages, carports, patio covers, and other accessory structures
  • Retaining walls and swimming pools when regulated
  • Roof replacements and interior finishes that fall under permit thresholds
  • Change of occupancy or use in some cases

Trade Permits and Integrated Applications

Separate or coordinated permits are often required for:

  • Electrical work
  • Mechanical (HVAC) systems
  • Plumbing work

For some residential and multifamily projects, county guidance describes integrated building permit applications where applicants select the disciplines included in the project. Confirm whether your scope should be filed as a single integrated application or as separate trade permits.

Related Approvals

These may also apply:

  • Municipal zoning, site plan, or occupancy approvals before county building permit filing
  • Occupancy inspections through the county portal
  • Water / sewer, transportation, or health reviews when triggered by the project
  • Demolition approvals in some circumstances

Typical Permit Process

While details vary by project and municipality, the general process often includes:

  1. Confirm county vs. municipal building authority and any required local zoning approval
  2. Prepare application materials, site information, and drawings
  3. Submit through Accela Citizen Access (or other required county / municipal channels)
  4. Plan review for building code compliance
  5. Respond to review comments or revisions if needed
  6. Permit issuance and fee payment
  7. Inspections during construction
  8. Final approval or certificate / occupancy closeout

Larger or more complex projects may involve review by multiple disciplines, fire safety coordination, and utility or transportation approvals. Commercial tenant finishes commonly need clear business / tenant information on the application.

Contractor Licensing

Contractors performing work under St. Louis County permitting are generally expected to hold appropriate county licensing where required. The Accela portal also supports contractor licensing applications. Permit processing may be delayed if contractor credentials are incomplete or if the wrong license is attached to the application. County guidance commonly advises applicants not to add subcontractor licenses to the building permit record during filing — those are handled separately when processed. Homeowners may perform some work on their own property in limited circumstances, but they remain responsible for obtaining required permits and passing inspections. Verify current licensing rules before scheduling work.

Projects That Commonly Require Permits

Examples of work that typically require permits include:

  • Home additions or structural remodels
  • Removal or modification of load-bearing elements
  • New commercial or multifamily construction
  • Interior tenant finishes and change-of-use projects
  • HVAC system installations or major modifications
  • Electrical service upgrades and EV charger installations
  • Plumbing system changes
  • Decks, retaining walls, pools, and accessory buildings
  • Roofing replacements (depending on scope)
  • Solar panel installations
  • Window or opening changes affecting structure or egress

Even projects that appear minor may require permits if they affect building systems, structural elements, or zoning compliance.

Work That May Not Require a Permit

Some minor work may not require a county building permit, such as:

  • Cosmetic finishes (painting, flooring, trim)
  • Minor repairs that do not affect structure or systems
  • Like-for-like fixture replacements in some cases

St. Louis County does not always publish one short consolidated exempt-work list that covers every edge case. Municipal zoning approval may still be required even when a county building permit is not. Confirm with the applicable city and county offices before beginning work when scope is unclear.

When Engineered Plans May Be Required

Plan reviewers may require drawings prepared or sealed by a licensed Professional Engineer in situations such as:

  • Structural modifications or load-bearing changes
  • Large spans, unusual framing, or non-prescriptive design
  • Commercial, industrial, or multifamily buildings
  • Unusual site, foundation, or retaining wall conditions
  • Mechanical, electrical, or plumbing systems beyond standard residential scope
  • Projects requiring fire protection or life-safety analysis

Engineered plans can help demonstrate compliance with applicable codes and support the permit review process.

How a Professional Engineer Can Help

Preparing Permit-Ready Documents

Engineers can provide drawings and calculations that meet local submission requirements for structural, mechanical, or electrical scope.

Evaluating Existing Structures

For remodels or additions, engineering review can help determine whether existing framing, foundations, or connections can support new loads.

Resolving Plan Review Comments

If county or municipal reviewers request revisions, an engineer can address technical concerns and provide supplemental documentation.

Supporting Non-Standard Designs

When a project falls outside prescriptive code provisions, engineering analysis can demonstrate equivalent performance.

Reducing Delays

Clear, complete engineering documentation submitted through Accela — after confirming the correct jurisdiction and record type — can help streamline approval and reduce back-and-forth during review.

Inspections and Compliance

After a permit is issued, inspections are typically required at various stages of construction, including:

  • Foundation or footing
  • Framing and structural connections
  • Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing rough-ins
  • Insulation or envelope components when applicable
  • Final inspection

Inspections are generally scheduled through Accela after the permit is issued. County guidance commonly notes that inspections may be scheduled several business days in advance — confirm current scheduling windows in the portal. Approval confirms that work matches permitted plans and applicable code requirements.

Practical Tips for Applicants

To help your project move smoothly in St. Louis County:

  • Confirm whether the county, a municipality, or the City of St. Louis is the building authority for your address
  • Obtain municipal zoning or related approvals when required before county filing
  • Choose the correct Accela construction record type on the first attempt
  • Register or confirm contractor licensing before permit submission when required
  • Submit clear, complete plans through the portal
  • Coordinate engineering needs before the first submittal
  • Respond promptly to review comments
  • Schedule inspections at required stages — not only at completion
  • Keep permit and inspection records for future reference

Preparation can help avoid delays, stop-work situations, and unexpected revisions during construction.

Closing

Building permit requirements in St. Louis County, Missouri vary depending on project scope, whether the property is unincorporated, and whether a municipality contracts with the county for code enforcement. Understanding Accela Citizen Access, confirming jurisdiction early, and preparing appropriate documentation can help streamline approval. In some cases, involvement from a Professional Engineer can help clarify technical issues and support code-compliant design.
