Deferred Submittals and Shop Drawing Review in Missouri: How PE Involvement Speeds Permits

August 27, 2026

Missouri commercial and larger residential projects often cannot wait until every connection, curtain wall, truss package, or specialty system is fully designed before applying for a building permit. Deferred submittals and shop drawings let construction start under an approved permit set while certain details are finalized later — but only when the process is managed clearly.

When deferred items pile up without engineering review, permits stall at resubmittal, inspections fail, or closeout drags. A licensed Professional Engineer (PE) may help by defining what can be deferred, reviewing shop drawings against the permitted design, and preparing documentation the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) expects.

This article provides general information about deferred submittals and shop drawing review in Missouri and how PE involvement can help keep permits moving. Requirements vary by city, county, and adopted code.

What Are Deferred Submittals?

A deferred submittal is construction documentation submitted after the initial permit approval for items that were not fully detailed in the first plan set. Common examples include:

  • Structural connections and specialty steel details
  • Pre-engineered metal building or truss packages
  • Curtain wall, storefront, and glazing systems
  • Firestopping and penetration details
  • Equipment anchorage and vibration isolation
  • Custom stairs, railings, or architectural metals

The building code framework used in Missouri typically allows deferred submittals when the design professional of record identifies them on the permit drawings and the AHJ accepts the approach. Deferred does not mean optional — it means the detail arrives later under a defined review path.

Shop Drawings vs. Permit Drawings

Permit drawings describe the overall design intent, code compliance strategy, and major systems. Shop drawings (or fabrication drawings) show how a supplier or contractor will actually build a specific component.

Important distinctions:

  • Shop drawings may include dimensions, connection details, and manufacturer-specific information not shown on the permit set
  • They should not silently change the permitted design without review
  • The engineer of record often reviews shop drawings for conformance with the permitted documents
  • Some jurisdictions also require AHJ review of certain deferred submittals before fabrication or installation

Confusing shop drawings with the permit set — or skipping PE review — is a frequent source of field corrections and inspection failures.

Why Deferred Submittals Exist

Deferred submittals serve practical project needs:

  • Long-lead specialty systems are not fully specified at permit time
  • Fabricators need awarded contracts before producing final details
  • Design-build or fast-track schedules require phased documentation
  • Tenant improvements depend on vendor selections made after lease signing

Used properly, deferrals let owners and contractors obtain permits and begin site work while preserving a controlled path for later technical approval. Used poorly, they create a backlog of unresolved items that blocks inspections, TCO, or certificate of occupancy closeout.

What Missouri AHJs Typically Expect

Local interpretation varies, but plan reviewers commonly look for:

  • A deferred submittal log or schedule on the permit drawings
  • Identification of each deferred item, responsible party, and submission timeline
  • Statement that deferred items will be submitted to the design professional for review before fabrication or installation
  • Indication of which items also require AHJ review
  • Minimum design criteria on the permit set so deferred details cannot drift from code intent

Some Missouri jurisdictions treat deferred submittals as a formal resubmittal with a separate review cycle. Others rely heavily on the engineer of record stamp and only spot-check at inspection. Knowing the local rule early avoids assuming a fabricator approval is enough.

How PE Involvement Speeds Permits

Professional Engineer involvement can shorten delays at several points:

Upfront permit strategy

A PE can identify which items are reasonable to defer, which must be on the initial submittal, and what minimum criteria the permit set must show so reviewers approve the deferral list the first time.

Shop drawing review

Timely PE review of supplier and contractor submittals catches conflicts with the permitted design before material is ordered or installed — reducing rework and inspection holds.

Resubmittal and comment response

When the AHJ comments on a deferred submittal, a PE can prepare a technical response, revised details, or a letter explaining conformance with the original design basis.

Inspection and closeout support

Inspectors may ask for evidence that deferred items were reviewed and approved. PE documentation helps close the loop for TCO and final occupancy, especially on projects with many specialty systems.

Common Deferred Submittal Categories

Structural and metal building

Truss layouts, PEMB anchor and bracing details, connection plates, and stair or mezzanine shop drawings often arrive after the shell permit. Engineering review confirms loads, reactions, and code paths match the permitted structural design.

Envelope and glazing

Curtain wall and storefront systems need deferred submittals showing anchorage, thermal breaks, and water management. Reviewers check that delegated design does not undermine the permitted envelope performance.

MEP and fire protection

Equipment schedules, duct/pipe layouts above ceilings, and fire suppression shop drawings may defer when the permit set shows design criteria. PE or specialty engineer review keeps deferred MEP aligned with permitted loads and life-safety assumptions.

Listed assemblies and product submittals

Products with evaluation reports (such as ICC-ES ESRs) may still require shop-level detailing for a specific project. PE review confirms the installed configuration matches the listing and the permit documents.

Typical Shop Drawing Review Workflow

  1. Permit set identifies deferred items and design criteria
  2. Contractor or supplier prepares shop drawing submittal
  3. Design team (often including PE review) checks conformance with permitted documents
  4. Submittal marked approved, approved as noted, or rejected for revision
  5. If required, AHJ receives deferred submittal for plan review
  6. Fabrication or installation proceeds after approvals
  7. Inspection verifies installed work matches approved submittals

Bottlenecks usually appear when step 3 is skipped, step 5 is assumed unnecessary, or submittals sit in a contractor queue without a deadline tied to the construction schedule.

Common Pitfalls That Slow Permits

  • No deferred submittal list on the initial permit drawings
  • Shop drawings that change structural or life-safety assumptions without PE review
  • Treating manufacturer catalog cuts as shop drawings without project-specific details
  • Installing deferred work before AHJ or engineer approval
  • Losing track of which deferred items remain open at inspection
  • Multiple revision cycles because submittals lack reactions, loads, or connection data the PE needs to review

How a Missouri Professional Engineer May Help

A PE supporting deferred submittals and shop drawing review may:

  • Prepare or review permit drawings that define deferral scope and criteria
  • Review structural, connection, or specialty submittals for conformance with the design
  • Stamp or document approval when responsible charge and scope require it
  • Respond to AHJ comments on deferred resubmittals
  • Coordinate with architects, fabricators, and contractors so submittals do not contradict the permitted set

Shop drawing review is generally a review service — confirming conformance with the engineer's design — rather than redesign of the entire system unless scope is expanded. Sealing and responsible charge depend on the assignment and jurisdiction.

Practical Guidance for Owners and Contractors

  • Ask the AHJ early which deferred items require jurisdiction review vs. engineer review only
  • Include a deferred submittal schedule in the first permit submittal, not after comments
  • Set internal deadlines for shop drawings tied to fabrication lead times
  • Submit complete packages — loads, reactions, and references — to avoid review ping-pong
  • Track open deferred items alongside inspection and TCO checklists
  • Budget PE review time in the schedule; it is usually cheaper than field rework

Closing

Deferred submittals and shop drawing review are normal parts of Missouri construction — not signs of a weak permit set when managed deliberately. Clear deferral lists, timely PE review, and aligned AHJ expectations keep permits moving, reduce inspection surprises, and support faster closeout.
